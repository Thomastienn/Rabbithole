Board: ask by voice

Rabbithole-brd.11. The mic in the board's tool group turns on hands-free voice questions. The first press downloads the speech models, with progress on the button, while typing still works. Once the mic is on, starting to speak stops the tutor, and a short pause sends the question. Speech recognition runs in the local server, so the learner's voice never leaves the machine. Spec: docs/board-spec.md. Decision: ADR 0028. The spec wins where they differ.

  • The mic is the 36px icon button first in the tool group, before speed and pause, with the microphone icon. It is off whenever a board opens, and one press turns it on. Its name stays "Turn mic on", and it is a toggle: pressed while on.
  • Off, it is the plain muted icon button. On, it fills porcelain, with the icon in the text-on-porcelain colour. That is all the mic shows while the tutor speaks: the tutor's own voice from the speakers never counts as the learner speaking.
  • When the learner speaks, the button widens to show four 3px level bars, 2px apart, 16px tall at most, to the right of the icon, and the state label reads "Listening". The tutor stops at once, and the item it was drawing finishes within about 150 ms.
  • About 300 ms of silence ends the question. The button narrows back to on, and the recognised words become the learner's message, shown muted on the caption line while the tutor thinks, as a typed question is. Only those words go to the provider.
  • The first press downloads Moonshine base and Silero voice detection, about 64 MB together. While they download, the button widens to show the percentage in 13px tabular figures, on the hover tone, with a 3px porcelain bar along its foot. A notice at the top of the board gives the size and says the voice stays on this computer. Typing and sending work meanwhile. Later presses turn the mic straight on, with no download.
  • When the download fails or the microphone is blocked, the mic goes back to off, and a failure notice in the scarlet wash says what to do. The next press tries again.
  • In the desktop app, the mic works the same, with no permission prompt: the app grants the mic to its own page.

4. Hearing the learner

The learner started talking. After about 150 ms of speech the tutor stops mid-sentence, the focus ring snaps to finished, and the cut sentence turns muted. The mic shows the learner's level, and the state reads Listening.

Listening

5. Question sent

About 300 ms of silence ended the question. It was recognised on this computer and sent as the learner's message, shown muted while the tutor thinks. The mic stays on, ready for the next cut-in.

Thinking

9. Microphone blocked

In a browser, the learner said no to the microphone prompt. The mic stays off, and the notice says where to allow it. The desktop app never shows this, because it grants its own page the mic.
