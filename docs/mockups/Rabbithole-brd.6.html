Flows, trees, tables and Venn diagrams grow one piece at a time

Rabbithole-brd.6. The remaining components, on the component layout from Rabbithole-brd.5. The tutor gives each one a box; the component places its own children and reflows the earlier ones inside the box when a new one arrives. Spec: docs/board-spec.md (Op language, Board player). Decision: ADR 0028. The spec wins where they differ.

  • Sizes are board units on the fixed 1600×900 board. Boxes are 5-unit ink strokes with 18-unit corners. Arrows, edges and chevrons are 4-unit muted strokes. Child text is 32 units in the reading face.
  • Flow: steps share the box width in equal columns, one per step so far. A step box is 110 high and as wide as its column less 104, at most 260. Each arrow stops 16 short of both boxes.
  • Tree: rows sit 240 apart from 80 below the top of the box, and tighten evenly when a deeper row would not fit. The root spans the box width, and each node splits its parent's span equally among its children. A node box is 84 high and its label's width plus 60.
  • Table: the header row is 70 high, with 28-unit muted 600 headings and a 3-unit strong-seam rule under it. Rows share the rest of the box, at most 110 each, with 2-unit seams between them. Columns are equal.
  • Venn: two or three ink circles, drawn one at a time, each with its muted set name outside it. Each item goes in the region its sets name. The items in one region stack 60 apart around its centre.
  • Whenever a child arrives, the earlier children slide to their new places while it draws on. Nothing jumps, and nothing leaves the box.
  • Captions show the sentence being spoken. Frames are read left to right, top to bottom. Dashed strokes on a board are mockup markers only.

2. Flow: one step and its arrow at a time

Each new step's arrow draws on from the step before, then its box draws on and its label writes in. As it arrives, the earlier steps slide left into their narrower columns.

> It starts with ripe grapes.
f1 flow at 800,430 w=1400 h=260
s1 step on f1 Grapes
> They're crushed into juice and skins.
s2 step on f1 Crushed
> Yeast turns the sugar into alcohol.
s3 step on f1 Ferment
> Then it rests in barrels for months.
s4 step on f1 Aged
> And that's wine.
s5 step on f1 Wine

The op lines are as the tutor writes them. The spec fixes at, w= h= and on; the other arguments here and below are illustrative, and the op parser owns their exact syntax.

3. Tree: one node and its edge at a time

Each new node's edge draws down from its parent, then its box draws on and its label writes in. A new sibling splits the parent's span again, so the earlier siblings slide aside.

> All cats are one family.
t1 tree at 800,450 w=1200 h=640
c0 node on t1 Cats
> Big cats are one branch.
c1 node on t1 under=c0 Big cats
> Small cats are the other.
c2 node on t1 under=c0 Small cats
> Lions are big cats.
c3 node on t1 under=c1 Lion
> So are tigers.
c4 node on t1 under=c1 Tiger
> House cats are small cats.
c5 node on t1 under=c2 House cat
> So are lynx.
c6 node on t1 under=c2 Lynx

4. Table: one row at a time

The table's headings and the rule under them draw in its first sentence, with the first row. Each later row writes in below, left cell first. When a row would not fit at 110, every row tightens evenly to share the box.

> Earth spins in a day and goes round the Sun in 365.
p1 table at 800,450 w=1100 h=420 cols=Planet|One spin|One year
r1 row on p1 Earth|1 day|365 days
> Mars spins in about a day too, but its year is 687.
r2 row on p1 Mars|1 day|687 days
> Mercury takes 59 days to spin, and 88 to go round.
r3 row on p1 Mercury|59 days|88 days
> Venus takes 243 days to spin, longer than its year.
r4 row on p1 Venus|243 days|225 days

5. Venn: one circle at a time, then the items

The venn op draws its circles one after another over its sentence, each followed by its set name. Items come after, one per sentence, each writing in at its region. A second item in a region pushes the first one up, so the pair stays centred.

> Birds on the left, things that fly on the right.
v1 venn at 800,470 w=1100 h=620 sets=Birds|Can fly
> Penguins are birds that can't fly.
i1 item on v1 in=Birds Penguin
> A sparrow is both.
i2 item on v1 in=Birds,Can fly Sparrow
> Bats fly, but they aren't birds.
i3 item on v1 in=Can fly Bat
> Ostriches can't fly either.
i4 item on v1 in=Birds Ostrich
> Let's sort them by what they can do.
v1 venn at 800,480 w=1100 h=800 sets=Lays eggs|Can fly|Swims
…
> A duck does all three.
i5 item on v1 in=Lays eggs,Can fly,Swims Duck
