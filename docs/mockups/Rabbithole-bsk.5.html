Board sketches: bars, pie, spectrum and quadrant build piece by piece

Rabbithole-bsk.5. Four quantity kinds of the chat's sketches come to the board, on the pattern of Rabbithole-bsk.4. The tutor gives each one a box; each child op adds one piece, one sentence at a time, and the sketch reflows the earlier pieces inside the box. The arrangement follows the chat sketch of the same name; every piece is drawn in the board's strokes, fonts and theme colours. Spec: docs/board-sketches-spec.md (Sketch kinds as board components). The spec wins where they differ.

  • Sizes are board units on the fixed 1600×900 board. Stations, lines, labels, notes, looks and reflow timing are Rabbithole-bsk.4's: 14-radius stations with a 5-unit ink edge, 4-unit muted lines, 32-unit ink labels, 28-unit muted notes.
  • Bars: labels start at the box's left; bars start 40 past the widest label and are 44 high with fully round ends and a 5-unit ink edge, no fill. The longest bar ends 220 before the box's right, leaving room for its value, which starts 24 past the bar's end. Rows at most 96 apart, centred.
  • Pie: the ring's outer radius is the smaller of half the box's height less 20 and 22% of its width; the inner radius is 55% of the outer. It sits 20 inside the box's left. The legend starts 110 right of the ring: a 36-unit rounded swatch in the slice's fill, the label 60 from the swatch's left, and the muted share 32 past the widest label. Legend rows at most 80 apart, centred. The first slice starts at the top.
  • Spectrum: the line spans the box, 100 below its top. Places sit in equal slots; names 64 below the line, notes 40 under the name. From five places, every second one drops to 190 below the line.
  • Quadrant: axes stop 200 inside the box's sides and 60 inside its top and bottom. End names sit 24 past each end. Labels start 30 from their station's centre.
  • Past eight children, text drops to 26 units, stations to an 11 radius and bars to 36 high; the box stays the same size.
  • A child the board cannot draw (no such sketch, another kind, another page, a missing value, a negative bar or slice, a coordinate outside 0 to 10) is left out without any notice on the board.
  • Colours are the theme's: ink is text-primary, muted text-secondary, the ground the board surface, porcelain rabbithole-paper. Slice fills are ink at 16% and 6%; a focus slice is porcelain at 30%.
  • Captions show the sentence being spoken. Frames read left to right, top to bottom. Dashed boxes on a board are mockup markers only.

How fast is light?

Light is fast, but space is big. It reaches Mercury in about three minutes. Venus takes six. Earth about eight. Mars nearly thirteen. And Jupiter takes forty-three minutes.

Board pages list, as the tutor reads it (not drawn):
- How long sunlight takes (open): g1 bars: Mercury 3.2 min, Venus 6 min, Earth 8.3 min, Mars 12.7 min, Jupiter 43 min

2. Bars: one bar at a time

Each bar is one sentence: its label writes in, the bar draws on from the left, then its value writes in. Rows are equal, at most 96 apart, as a block centred in the box, so a new bar lifts the rest by half a row. A bigger value than any so far rescales every earlier bar: they shorten together while the new one draws.

> Light is fast, but space is big.
g1 bars at 800,450 w=1200 h=520
> It reaches Mercury in about three minutes.
b1 bar "Mercury" value=3.2 unit="min" in g1
> Venus takes six.
b2 bar "Venus" value=6 unit="min" in g1
…
b5 bar "Jupiter" value=43 unit="min" in g1

Op names and arguments are the spec's; the op parser owns their exact syntax and a later benchmark may tune them. Dashed boxes are mockup markers for where a child was before the reflow. A negative value is skipped and logged, and draws nothing.

3. Pie: one slice at a time

A ring at the left of the box with a legend to its right. Each slice is one sentence: it sweeps clockwise from where the last one ended, then its legend row writes in with its share. Slices are told apart by their ink edges and fills alternating 16% and 6% ink, never by hue. Shares are of the slices drawn so far, so they change as slices arrive; values that add up to 100 show as written.

> Let's see what air is made of.
a1 pie at 800,450 w=1200 h=600
> Most of the air is nitrogen, 78 percent.
s1 slice "Nitrogen" value=78 in a1
> Oxygen is most of the rest, 21.
s2 slice "Oxygen" value=21 in a1
> And the last one percent is argon and a few others.
s3 slice "Argon and others" value=1 in a1

4. Spectrum: one place at a time, in order

The spectrum op draws the line across the box with its two ends written above it, muted. Each place is one sentence: a station on the line, its name below and a muted note under that. Places share the line in equal slots in the order given, so each new one slides the others left. From five places, every second one drops to a lower row on a 3-unit muted leader, so labels never collide.

> Everything sits somewhere between acid and alkaline.
h1 spectrum ends="acid..alkaline" at 800,450 w=1300 h=440
> Lemon juice is very acidic, about pH 2.
q1 place "Lemon juice" note="pH 2" in h1
…
q5 place "Bleach" note="pH 13" in h1

5. Quadrant: one spot at a time

The quadrant op draws two muted axes crossing at the box's middle, with arrowheads at the high ends and the four end names, muted, past each end. Each spot is one sentence: a station at its coordinates, from 0 to 10 on each axis, and its label to the right, or to the left past 7 across. Spots never move. A second spot at the same coordinates shares the station, its label 44 below the first.

> Let's place power sources by cost and by how clean they are.
k1 quadrant x="cheap..costly" y="dirty..clean" at 800,450 w=1300 h=780
> Coal is cheap, but the dirtiest.
m1 spot "Coal" 2,1 in k1
…
> Solar and wind are now both cheap and clean.
m4 spot "Solar" 3,9 in k1
m5 spot "Wind" 3,9 in k1

A coordinate below 0 or above 10 is skipped and logged, and draws nothing.

6. More than eight pieces still fit the box

Past eight children the text drops from 32 to 26 units and the bars from 44 to 36 high, and the rows tighten to share the same box. The box never grows and no piece leaves it.

7. Reaching one piece

Every bar, slice, place and spot is a board item with its own id, so the tutor highlights, points at and erases one piece, and the learner circles one with the pen.
