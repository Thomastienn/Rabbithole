Board sketches: cycle, stack, timeline and parts build piece by piece

Rabbithole-bsk.4. The first four of the chat's sketch kinds come to the board as components. The tutor gives each one a box; each child op adds one piece, one sentence at a time, and the sketch reflows the earlier pieces inside the box. The arrangement follows the chat sketch of the same name; every piece is drawn in the board's strokes, fonts and theme colours. Spec: docs/board-sketches-spec.md (Sketch kinds as board components). Frame from Rabbithole-pgs.4, components from Rabbithole-brd.6. The spec wins where they differ.

  • Sizes are board units on the fixed 1600×900 board. Stations are 14-radius circles with a 5-unit ink edge, filled with the board's ground. Loops, lines, buses and chevrons are 4-unit muted strokes; chevron arms are 16 long. Shelves are 3-unit border-emphasized rules. Labels are 32 units in the reading face, in ink; notes are 28 units, muted.
  • Cycle: the loop is a rounded rectangle 40 inside the box's sides, with 48-unit corners, its top run 50 below the box's top and its bottom run 90 above its bottom. The top run holds the first half of the stages, rounded up, in equal columns left to right; the bottom run holds the rest, right to left. Labels sit 41 below their station's centre. One chevron sits midway between each pair of stages, or midway down the side where the loop turns.
  • Stack: rows at most 100 apart, centred in the box. The name starts at the box's left; the note column starts 48 past the widest name. The shelf spans the box, 6 above the row's bottom.
  • Timeline: rows at most 110 apart, centred in the box. The line runs 40 right of the widest date. Dates end 24 left of the station's edge, labels start 24 right of it.
  • Parts: the whole's station sits 40 past its name, at the box's middle. The bus is 80 right of it and each part's station 80 right of the bus. Names start 24 past a part's station; notes start 40 past the widest name. Rows at most 110 apart, centred on the whole.
  • Whenever a child arrives, the earlier children slide and resize to their new places over 400 ms while the new one draws on; with reduced motion they move at once and the new one fades in. Nothing leaves the box.
  • Past eight children, text drops to 26 units and stations to an 11 radius; the box stays the same size.
  • A child the board cannot draw (no such sketch, another kind, another page, a missing label) is left out without any notice on the board.
  • Colours are the theme's: ink is text-primary, muted text-secondary, the ground the board surface, porcelain rabbithole-paper. A theme or appearance switch redraws every sketch in the new colours.
  • Captions show the sentence being spoken. Frames read left to right, top to bottom. Dashed boxes on a board are mockup markers only.

What is the water cycle?

Water is always moving in a loop. The sun warms the sea, and water rises as vapour. High up it cools into clouds. Then it falls as rain or snow. And it runs into the sea, where it all starts again.

Board pages list, as the tutor reads it (not drawn):
- The water cycle (open): w1 cycle: Evaporation, Condensation, Precipitation, Collection

2. Cycle: one stage at a time

Each stage is one sentence. A new stage's chevron draws on from the stage before, then its station and its label. The top run holds the first half of the stages, rounded up, so a new stage can move earlier ones: the third stage pushes the second down to the bottom run. The loop draws with the second stage and never redraws; the last stage brings the chevron back to the first.

> Water is always moving in a loop.
w1 cycle at 800,450 w=1200 h=560
> The sun warms the sea, and water rises as vapour.
c1 stage "Evaporation" in w1
> High up it cools into clouds.
c2 stage "Condensation" in w1
> Then it falls as rain or snow.
c3 stage "Precipitation" in w1
> And it runs into the sea, where it all starts again.
c4 stage "Collection" in w1

Op names and arguments are the spec's; the op parser owns their exact syntax and a later benchmark may tune them. Dashed boxes are mockup markers for where a child was before the reflow.

3. Stack: one layer at a time, top first

Each layer is a name in ink and a muted note, on a shelf as wide as the box. The layers sit in equal rows, at most 100 apart, as a block centred in the box, so each new layer below lifts the ones above by half a row. The notes line up in one column, 48 past the widest name so far.

> Your browser talks through layers.
n1 stack at 800,450 w=1100 h=600
> At the top is HTTP, the page you asked for.
l1 layer "HTTP" note="the page you asked for" in n1
> Under it, TLS keeps it secret.
l2 layer "TLS" note="keeps it secret" in n1
…
l5 layer "Ethernet" note="the wire or the Wi-Fi" in n1

4. Timeline: one event at a time, in order

Events go down one line in the order the tutor gives them; the board never sorts or spaces them by date. Each event is a station on the line, its date in ink to the left and its label to the right. Rows are equal, at most 110 apart, as a block centred in the box. Each new event draws the line down from the event before.

> English starts when the Anglo-Saxons arrive, around 450.
y1 timeline at 800,450 w=1000 h=640
e1 event "Anglo-Saxons arrive" when="450" in y1
> In 1066 the Normans conquer, and French moves in.
e2 event "Normans conquer" when="1066" in y1
…
e5 event "Johnson's dictionary" when="1755" in y1

5. Parts: the whole first, then one part at a time

The parts op draws the whole: a station and its name at the left of the box, at its middle. Each part leaves a bus at right angles: a station, its name in ink and a muted note. The first part draws the stub from the whole to the bus; each later one lengthens the bus to reach it. Parts sit in equal rows, at most 110 apart, centred on the whole.

> A neuron has four main parts.
r1 parts whole="A neuron" at 800,450 w=1300 h=560
> Dendrites take signals in.
p1 part "Dendrites" note="take signals in" in r1
…
p4 part "Synapse" note="passes it on" in r1

6. More than eight pieces still fit the box

Past eight children the text drops from 32 to 26 units and stations from 14 to 11, and the pieces share the same box. The box never grows and no piece leaves it. The prompt asks for about eight at most, so this is the rare case.

7. Reaching one piece

Every stage, layer, event and part is a board item with its own id, so the tutor highlights, points at and erases one piece, and the learner circles one with the pen, as with a flow's steps.

Looks work on a single piece too: look=focus draws it in porcelain, look=muted in the secondary text colour.

8. Looks on single pieces

A piece drawn with look=focus is porcelain, station and text; look=muted is the secondary text colour. The rest of the sketch keeps the ink.
