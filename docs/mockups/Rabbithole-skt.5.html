Sketches build in and fit every reply layout

Rabbithole-skt.5. A sketch fades in one item after another over 0.5 s, in step with the reply's reveal, and appears at once with reduced motion. It sits anywhere a paragraph can, in every reply layout, and fits each at the widths the app really gives it. The flow and bars shapes are skt.1's and do not change here. Spec: docs/sketches-spec.md. Decision: ADR 0027. The spec wins where they differ.

  • A sketch lays out at its own box's width, at scale 1. Its text never shrinks or grows (title 15px, labels 14px, arrow labels 13px) and its lines stay 3px. The box is the column or layout cell the sketch sits in, at most 640px. Its breakpoints are container widths, never window widths.
  • Flow stays horizontal while each rank column (box width divided by ranks) is at least 120px, or at least 160px when the flow forks. Below that it turns vertical. A 4-rank flow with a fork is horizontal only at 640px. A straight 4-rank flow is horizontal from 480px.
  • Vertical flow runs down a 3px line 8px from the left edge. A fork leaves its station 12px down and turns 45 degrees onto a lane 24px to the right. A rejoin leaves its station 12px down and turns 45 degrees back, merging 36px above its target so the two steps share one chevron. Labels start 20px right of the last lane. Stations are 44px apart, plus 18px per extra label line and 20px for an arrow label, and 72px before a rejoin. Chevrons point down at the middle of each step, and an arrow label sits beside its chevron.
  • Labels and arrow labels wrap at word breaks: in horizontal flow at the column width minus 24px, in vertical flow at the box's right edge. A word longer than its line breaks inside the word. The drawing grows taller and never overflows its box or scrolls.
  • Bars keep their labels beside the bars from 280px. The label column takes its widest label, up to 45% of the box, and the track is at most 360px. Below 280px each label sits on its own line, 2px above its bar, and rows are 10px apart. The longest bar is always the track minus 72px, which leaves room for the value.
  • Inside a layout, the layout's spacing replaces the sketch's own: 12px between it and its neighbours in a card, entry or column, and none at the cell's top or bottom. Station rings are filled with the surface under them: the ground, or raised enamel inside a comparison panel or a callout. A sketch counts toward a figure's text height, so a tall one can make the figure stack, as long text already does.
  • Below a 480px reply, every layout already renders as plain flow (LAYOUT_MIN_WIDTH), so there a sketch sits at the full column width. The title always stops 8px short of the controls box, whether the box shows or not, so pointing at a sketch never covers or reflows the title. Below a 400px box the controls show only the photo icon, still named "Expand into visual", so the title gives up 44px instead of 164px. A title that wraps is balanced across its lines. Where the device cannot hover, the controls always show, so they sit 8px under the sketch at its right instead of over its title.
  • Build-in: each item fades from opacity 0 to 1 over 160ms on the house ease-out, cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1). An item is its station or bar together with its label, its value, its incoming line, its chevron and its arrow label. Item i starts at i × min(120ms, 340ms ÷ (n − 1)). Five items start 85ms apart and four start 113ms apart, and both end at 500ms. The title fades in with the first item. Items go in reading order: ranks left to right with the top row before the fork row, and bars top to bottom.
  • Only opacity moves. There is no line draw, slide, scale or growing bar: the One Motion Rule keeps the line draw for new branches. The sketch takes its full height from the first frame, so nothing around it moves.
  • In step with the reveal, the build starts when the fence closes and the sketch replaces its placeholder. The text after it keeps streaming and does not wait. A sketch that is already complete when it first shows draws at once, like the text around it. This covers a reopened or scrolled-back reply, a theme switch and a check answer opened later.
  • With reduced motion (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) there is no build. The sketch draws whole at 0ms as the placeholder goes. Screen readers get the whole figure and its item list at 0ms either way, because the build is visual only.

Build-in, live

A reply streaming in at an illustrative speed. While each fence is open its placeholder holds the place. When the fence closes, the sketch builds and the next paragraph streams at the same time. Tick Reduced motion to see the sketches appear at once. Your system's reduced-motion setting does the same.

Counting arrangements

Flip four coins and write down the order: there are 16 possible sequences. Most of them land in the middle. There is only one way to get no heads, but six ways to get exactly two.

Entropy counts those ways. A state that can happen in more ways is more likely, which is why mixed results win.

Why freezing water does not break the rule

Ice is more ordered than water, so the water's own entropy falls as it freezes. But freezing releases heat, and that heat spreads into colder surroundings, which gain more entropy than the water lost.

So the total still rises. The second law is about the whole system, never one part of it.

Reduced motion

With prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, the placeholder gives way to the finished sketch in one frame. There is no fade, no stagger and no delay, and the layout is the same as with the build on.

Each degree of latitude you move north, the ground under you travels a shorter circle.

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { sketch items: no animation, opacity 1 at 0 ms }
