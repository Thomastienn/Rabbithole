Graphs and number lines build up on the board

Rabbithole-brd.5. The first board components: the tutor gives a box, and the component lays out its own children inside it, one piece per sentence. Spec: docs/board-spec.md (Op language, Board player). Decision: ADR 0028. The spec wins where they differ.

  • Everything on the board is in board units on the fixed 1600×900 board, which scales to the content width. Sizes below are board units.
  • Axes are 4-unit muted strokes with 26-unit muted tick numbers, all inside the axes' box. A plot is a 6-unit ink marker stroke in the axes' own units, traced left to right over its sentence.
  • A number line is a 4-unit muted line with ticks and 26-unit muted numbers under it. A point is an 11-unit ink dot that grows, then its 36-unit label writes in 50 units above it.
  • A point label that would touch a neighbour's goes to a second row 60 units higher with a 3-unit muted leader. Labels in a crowded run alternate rows in value order, so an earlier label may move. It slides there while the new point grows.
  • Captions show the sentence being spoken. Frames are read left to right, top to bottom. Dashed strokes and italic notes on a board are mockup markers only.

2. Axes first, then each curve traces left to right

The axes draw on with their ticks and numbers in their own sentence. Each plot then traces from its left end to its right end over the sentence that names it, in the axes' units. Its label writes in after the curve, in the same sentence.

> Let's set up axes: x from 0 to 5, y up to 32.
a1 axes at 680,480 w=1080 h=680 x=0..5 y=0..32
> This is x squared, tracing up from zero.
p1 plot on a1 y=x^2
t1 text at 1265,294 x²
> Now two to the x, which starts at one.
p2 plot on a1 y=2^x
t2 text at 1265,164 2ˣ
> They meet at 2 and at 4, then two to the x pulls away.

The op lines are as the tutor writes them. The spec fixes at, w= h= and on; the axis ranges and the plot's function form are illustrative, and the op parser owns their exact syntax.

3. A number line, then one point at a time

The line draws on left to right with its ticks, then each point grows at its value and its label writes in. When two thirds lands 40 units from 0.7, their labels would touch, so 0.7's label slides up to the second row on a leader while the new point grows. Three quarters is far enough from two thirds to share the first row.

> Let's put them on a line from zero to one.
n1 numberline at 800,500 w=1300 h=240 range=0..1 ticks=0,1/2,1
> Here's 0.7.
q1 point on n1 0.7 label=0.7
> Two thirds is just to its left.
q2 point on n1 2/3 label=2/3
> Three quarters is further right, so 0.7 sits closer to two thirds.
q3 point on n1 3/4 label=3/4

A point's value is in the line's units. The range, tick and label arguments are illustrative, as above.

4. To the guard, a component is one item at its box

The guard checks the axes as a single item, the box the tutor gave, not each tick, curve or number inside it. The component keeps its own children inside the box, so they never count as overlaps. The labels the tutor placed to the right are ordinary items and clear the box, so nothing moves.

5. A child naming an unknown parent

The tutor writes a plot on a2, but the board has no a2. The op is skipped and logged. Nothing is drawn, no error or op text shows, and the caption carries on with the sentence.

> Cubes would climb faster still.
p3 plot on a2 y=x^3
board: skipped op p3 (unknown parent a2): p3 plot on a2 y=x^3

The second block is the server log, never the board. Its wording is illustrative; the broken-op logging from Rabbithole-brd.2 owns the format.
