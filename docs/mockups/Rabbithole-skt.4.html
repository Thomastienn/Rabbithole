Sketches: plot, sequence, equation, grid

Rabbithole-skt.4. Four more kinds for the sketch module, drawn in the same way as flow and bars in Rabbithole-skt.1: on the reply's own ground, the moment the fence closes. Spec: docs/sketches-spec.md. Decision: ADR 0027. The spec wins where they differ.

  • Everything in Rabbithole-skt.1 still holds: no frame, fill or head band; at most 640px wide, 8px below the paragraph before and 20px above the next; only ink, muted ink, the strong seam and porcelain; a 15px/600 title; no Fig N, copy button or source.
  • The focus is porcelain. When the stars wrap a whole label, the whole label is 600-weight porcelain. When they wrap only part of it, as in 19^6 mod 23 = *2*, only the starred words are, and the item's mark (ring, line, leader or cell) turns porcelain.
  • Maths uses the app's maths renderer (KaTeX, as MathML in the browser's math font): the formula of an equation, its symbols, the curve labels of a plot and a grid's join signs. Every other word is in the reading face. Labels outside an equation or plot are text, so a ^ there stays as written.
  • Plot: a 640 × 216 drawing. The plot area starts 40px from the left and 8px from the top and is 176px tall. Its right edge leaves 12px plus the widest curve label. The axes are 1.5px muted lines at zero, or at the area's low edge when zero is outside the range. There are no gridlines and no tick marks. Tick labels are 12px muted with tabular figures: y labels end 8px left of the area, x labels are centred 20px under it. There are 4 to 6 even steps, plus each end of x: when it is 40px or more from a step. The y range runs from 0 or the lowest value, whichever is lower, to the highest value, rounded out to a step.
  • Plot curves are 3px strong-seam lines with round caps, sampled 200 times across x:; the focus curve is porcelain. A curve breaks where its value is not a number. Each curve is named at its right end, 12px past the area, in 15px maths. Two names closer than 20px are pushed apart to 20px.
  • Sequence: actors share the width evenly, each in a column 640px / n wide, its name in 14px ink centred at the top. Lifelines are 2px strong seam from 26px down to the last row. Rows follow the source order. A message row is 44px: the label baseline sits 18px down and the line 30px down. A note row is 32px, plus 18px for each extra line.
  • A message is a 3px strong-seam line from lifeline to lifeline, with a 2px muted chevron whose tip sits 8px before the receiver. Its 14px ink label is centred above it and wraps at the span minus 16px. A note is a 12px muted ring on its actor's lifeline. Its text is 14px and starts 14px right of the lifeline (on the last actor it ends 14px to the left), wrapping at the column minus 28px. Where a message crosses a lifeline, the lifeline breaks from 26px above the line to 6px below, so the label never sits on it.
  • Equation: the formula sits on one 40px row in 26px maths, left aligned, one symbol per space-separated token, 10px apart. Each annotated symbol drops a 2px strong-seam leader 14px plus 26px per row, then turns right for 10px. Its meaning is 14px ink, 6px past the turn and centred on it. The rightmost annotated symbol takes the top row, so no leader crosses a meaning. A symbol with no meaning line has no leader. The height is 38px + 26px × rows.
  • Grid: cells sit on a 1px strong-seam lattice, outer edge included, with square corners. Each cell has 14px text, 6px × 10px padding, and is at least 36 × 32px. A cell wider than 200px wraps. A grid of only numbers is a matrix, its cells centred with tabular figures. A grid with any word in it is a table: its cells are left aligned and its first row is the header, in 13px/600 muted with a 2px rule under it.
  • A name: caption is 13px muted, 6px above its grid. A join sign (×, =, + or →) is 24px muted maths with 16px on each side, centred on the grids. Grids with no join between them stack 16px apart. A focus cell has 600-weight porcelain text and a 2px porcelain ring inside the lattice. A joined row wider than the column scrolls sideways, as display maths does today.

In a reply: grid and plot

A table of numbers and the curve they lead to, each right after the text it shows. Both have a title and a focus.

Slope at a single point

To find the slope of y = x² at x = 1, take a second point a little to the right, at 1 + h, and work out the slope of the line through both. Then let h shrink.

The slope closes in on 2. That limit is the derivative at x = 1, and the line through (1, 1) with slope 2 is the tangent there.

Near x = 1 the curve and its tangent are almost the same line. Further away they part, which is why the derivative only describes one point.

In a reply: sequence and equation

Who sends what, then the formula behind it with the reply's own numbers.

Agreeing on a key in public

Alice and Bob agree in the open on a prime, 23, and a base, 5. Each picks a secret number and sends only 5 raised to it, mod 23.

An eavesdropper sees 23, 5, 8 and 19, but never 6 or 15. Both sides still reach the same number, because each raised the other's value to their own secret.

The 90 in the exponent is the secret nobody sent. Finding it from 8 and 19 alone is the hard problem that keeps the key safe.

A message from Alice to Bob passes over Eve's lifeline, which breaks under its label and line. A note on the middle actor sits to the right of its lifeline. Seven rows, in source order.

Equation: a subscript, a function name and the focus

Every symbol has a meaning, including the function name ln. The subscripted kB is one symbol. W has the focus, so its leader and its symbol are porcelain.

```sketch equation
title: Boltzmann's entropy
eq: S = k_B ln W
S: entropy, in joules per kelvin
k_B: 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K, sets the units
ln: turns multiplied counts into sums
*W*: microstates that fit
```

A sketch the app cannot draw

Nothing shows, for every kind. The paragraphs close up as if the block were not there: no gap, no error box, no source. The console logs the kind and the reason. A plot expression is parsed by its own grammar and never run as code, so a name outside it is an error.

ReLU keeps a positive input as it is and turns a negative one into zero.

What a screen reader hears

The figure is named "Sketch: title", or just "Sketch" with no title. Its items follow as a list, in source order, built from the items the sketch module returns. An equation's formula is read from its MathML. A grid is read as a table, row by row. The tutor writes no alt text.
