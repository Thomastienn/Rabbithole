Sketches: comparisons and quantities

Rabbithole-skt.3 adds six kinds: compare, venn, pie, line, quadrant and spectrum. They draw on the reply's own ground like flow and bars in skt.1, and keep its title, focus, controls, placeholder, hiding and screen-reader naming. Spec: docs/sketches-spec.md. Decision: ADR 0027. The spec wins where they differ.

  • Everything in skt.1 holds: no frame, fill or head band; at most 640px wide; 8px below the paragraph before and 20px above the next; a 15px/600 title; 14px labels in ink; 13px values, notes and axis labels in muted ink; all text in the reading face.
  • No line colours, and no gradient. Lines, rings and areas are the strong seam; the focus alone is porcelain, as a filled station, ring, slice or swatch and a 600-weight label. Pie slices are told apart by a 2px ground-coloured gap and by alternating full and half opacity, never by hue.
  • Compare is a three-column grid in 14px/20px text: the aspect column takes the width it needs up to 30%, the two value columns split the rest, 16px apart. Each name heads its column over a 3px round-ended strong-seam line, 6px below it. Rows have 7px above and below and a hairline seam between them; a value wraps in its column.
  • Venn is two 3px strong-seam ellipses, 320 × 160px at least (160px half-width, 80px half-height), centres 160px apart, so the overlap is the middle third. Items stack centred at x 160, 320 and 480, 20px a line and 6px between items, wrapping at 112px outside and 128px in the overlap. Each ellipse grows taller until every line clears both outlines by 8px. Set names sit 12px above, centred over each outer part, wrapping at 224px.
  • Pie is a ring 176px across, 32px thick (radii 80 and 48), slices clockwise from 12 o’clock in written order. The rows beside it, 24px from the ring, are 20px lines 6px apart: a 16 × 8px round swatch, the label (wrapping at 220px), the value right-aligned and the share in 13px muted ink. Shares round to whole percents, “<1%” below one. Values with no unit that sum to 100 are shown as written, with a %, and no value column.
  • Line is a 592px axis from x 24 to 616, with 14px muted end ticks. Values sit on 12px stations (the focus 14px filled porcelain); the label is 18px above the axis, the value 26px below. A crowded label rises in 22px rows, at most three rows, on a 1.5px strong-seam leader. The range ends are written under the ticks unless one would come within 12px of a value.
  • Quadrant is a 400 × 240px field mapped from 0 to 10 on both axes, crossed by 3px strong-seam axes at 5, 5, with a muted chevron at each high end. The y labels sit centred 14px above and 24px below the vertical axis; the x labels sit 12px beside the horizontal axis ends, wrapping at 88px. Item labels start 12px right of their station, or end 12px left of it past x 7, wrapping at 150px, with a 4px ground-coloured halo where they cross an axis.
  • Spectrum is a 3px strong-seam line from x 24 to 616, with its ends above it in muted ink, wrapping at 220px. Items are spaced evenly, centred in 592/n-px slots, in written order: a station on the line, the name 30px below, the note under it. From five items, every second item drops to a second row on a 1.5px leader.
  • A value out of its kind’s range is an error: a line value outside its range, a quadrant coordinate below 0 or above 10, or a negative pie share. So is a missing head line (vs:, ends:, or a second set). The reply hides the sketch and logs its kind and reason, as in skt.1.

Compare in a reply

The two names head their columns over a 3px strong-seam line. Each aspect is one row: the aspect in muted ink on the left, then each side’s value. Rows sit on hairline seams.

Why antibiotics miss a cold

A cold is caused by a virus, and a virus is not a smaller bacterium. Antibiotics jam machinery that only a living cell has, such as its ribosomes.

A virus borrows the host cell’s ribosomes instead, so there is nothing of its own for the drug to hit.

```sketch compare
title: Bacterium or virus
vs: Bacterium | Virus
Structure: A living cell | Genes in a protein shell
Typical size: About 1–2 micrometres | Mostly 20–300 nanometres
Own ribosomes: Yes | No
Reproduces: By itself | Only inside a host cell
Antibiotics: Often work | *Don't work*
```

Compare: long values, the pipe form, no title

An aspect or value wraps inside its column and the row grows; the three cells of a row share its top line. The aspect column takes the width it needs, up to 30%, and the two value columns split the rest evenly. The tutor sometimes writes aspect | A | B: a bar before any colon ends the aspect, and the row draws the same. With no title, the controls box sits over the top-right corner on hover, as for flow in skt.1.

```sketch compare
title: Europe’s rival blocs
vs: Triple Alliance | Triple Entente
Members | Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy | France, Russia, Britain
Formed | 1882 | 1894 to 1907, in stages
What each member promised to do | To defend the others if attacked | Talks first, no promise to fight
```
```sketch compare
vs: House | Senate
Voting members: 435 | 100
Seats: By state population | 2 per state
Term: 2 years | *6 years*
Special role: Starts revenue bills | Confirms nominees
```

Venn in a reply

Two outlined ellipses overlap by a third of their width. Each set’s own items sit in its outer part, the both items in the overlap, each stack centred. The set names sit above, in muted ink.

What every cell shares

Plant and animal cells are both eukaryotic: each keeps its DNA in a nucleus and makes its energy in mitochondria. The differences come from how they live.

A plant cannot move to find food, so it makes its own in chloroplasts and holds its shape with a rigid wall.

```sketch venn
title: Plant and animal cells
Plant cell: Cell wall, Chloroplasts, Large vacuole
Animal cell: Centrioles, Lysosomes
both: Nucleus, *Mitochondria*, Cell membrane, Ribosomes
```

Venn: long items, an empty side and a focus set

Items wrap at 112px in the outer parts and 128px in the overlap, and the ellipses grow taller until every line clears both outlines by 8px. A set with no items of its own leaves its outer part empty. A starred set name is porcelain, and so is its outline.

```sketch venn
title: Square or rectangle
*Squares*:
Rectangles: Sides of two different lengths, Only the opposite sides match
both: Four right angles, Opposite sides parallel and equal, Diagonals bisect each other
```

Pie in a reply

A 176px ring, 32px thick, beside its rows. Slices run clockwise from 12 o’clock in the order written, split by 2px gaps in the ground colour, and alternate strong seam at full and half opacity. Each row repeats its slice’s tone as a 16px line swatch, then the value as written and its share in muted ink.

Where a day’s energy comes from

A typical 2,000 kcal day splits across three macronutrients. Fat gives the smallest share by weight, but each gram carries 9 kcal, more than twice the 4 of carbohydrate or protein.

So a little fat goes a long way: 700 kcal is under 80 grams of it.

```sketch pie
title: A 2,000 kcal day
Carbohydrates: 1,000 kcal
*Fat*: 700 kcal
Protein: 300 kcal
```

Pie: eight slices that already add up to 100

When the values have no unit and sum to 100, they are the shares: each row shows the number as written with a % sign, and the value column is left out. A long label wraps at 220px and its row grows.

```sketch pie
title: Elements in the human body, by mass
Oxygen: 65
*Carbon*: 18.5
Hydrogen: 9.5
Nitrogen: 3.2
Calcium: 1.5
Phosphorus: 1
Potassium: 0.4
Everything else, mostly sulfur and sodium: 0.9
```

Pie: tiny shares and a zero

A share too thin to see still keeps its row and its percentage; the gaps swallow its slice. A computed share under 1% reads “<1%”. A zero draws no slice and reads 0%.

```sketch pie
title: Dry air, by volume
Nitrogen: 78.08
*Oxygen*: 20.95
Argon: 0.93
Carbon dioxide: 0.04
Water vapour: 0
```
```sketch pie
title: Who answered the survey
Students: 412 people
Teachers: 61 people
*Parents*: 127 people
Governors: 3 people
```

Line in a reply

A number line: a 3px strong-seam axis with 14px end ticks, a station per value, the label above it and the value below it in muted ink. The range ends are read at the tick ends.

The all-or-nothing spike

A neuron at rest sits near −70 mV. Inputs nudge it up a few millivolts at a time, and nothing happens until it reaches about −55 mV.

Past that threshold, sodium channels snap open and the voltage shoots up to around +30 mV in under a millisecond.

```sketch line
title: Membrane voltage (mV)
range: -90..50
-70: Resting
-55: *Threshold*
30: Typical peak
```

Line: close values stack, and a value on the end

A label that would come within 12px of its neighbour rises one 22px row, at most two, with a 1.5px strong-seam leader down to its station. A leader never crosses a label. A label near an end shifts inward to stay inside 640px. A value on the range end replaces that end’s number. A hyphen before a number draws as a minus sign.

```sketch line
title: What λ does to its eigenvector
range: -2..3
-1: Flips, same length
0: Squashed to zero
0.5: Shrinks
1: *Unchanged*
2: Doubles
3: Triples
```

Line: a unit on each value

A unit after the number, as in bars, follows the value under the station, and the range ends take the same unit. An end number that would come within 12px of a value is left out, as the 25 °C is here.

```sketch line
title: Food storage temperatures
range: -25..25
-18 °C: Freezer
4 °C: *Fridge upper limit*
21 °C: Typical room
```

Quadrant in a reply

Two 3px strong-seam axes cross at 5, 5 in a 400 × 240 field, each with a chevron at its high end. The axis labels sit at the axis ends in muted ink. Items are stations with the label to the right, or to the left past x 7; a 4px halo in the ground colour keeps a label readable where it crosses an axis.

Sorting a busy week

The Eisenhower matrix asks two separate questions of every task: does it have to happen soon, and does it matter? Urgent tasks shout, so they crowd out the important ones that do not.

Weekly revision sits top left: it never feels urgent, which is exactly why it needs a fixed slot.

```sketch quadrant
title: Urgent or important?
x: Not urgent .. Urgent
y: Not important .. Important
Exam tomorrow: 9, 9
*Weekly revision*: 2, 9
Booking the dentist: 4, 6
Most notifications: 8, 2
Scrolling feeds: 1, 1
```

Quadrant: edges, a shared spot and long axis labels

A 0 or a 10 sits on the field’s edge. Items at the same spot share one station and their labels stack 20px apart; a stack that would pass the field’s bottom grows upward instead. Axis labels wrap at 88px beside the horizontal axis ends.

```sketch quadrant
title: Power sources: cost and carbon
x: Cheap to build .. Costly to build
y: Low carbon .. High carbon
Coal: 4, 10
Gas: 3, 6
*Nuclear*: 10, 0
Onshore wind: 2, 0
Solar farms: 2, 0
Offshore wind: 6, 1
```

Spectrum in a reply

A 3px strong-seam line with its two ends written above it in muted ink. Items are spaced evenly, in the order written, each a station with its name below and its note under that in muted ink. No gradient: the order carries the scale.

Why obsidian is glass

Igneous rocks all start as molten rock. What changes is how fast it cools: slow cooling deep underground gives the atoms time to line up into large crystals.

Lava that hits air or water freezes before crystals can form at all, so obsidian is a natural glass.

```sketch spectrum
title: Cooling speed and crystal size
ends: Slow cooling .. Very fast cooling
Granite: crystals easy to see
Basalt: crystals mostly tiny
*Obsidian*: glass, no real crystals
```

Spectrum: seven items, long ends, an item with no note

Up to four items share one row, each wrapping at its slot width minus 16px. With five or more, every second item drops to a second row on a 1.5px strong-seam leader, and labels wrap at two slots minus 16px. Ends wrap at 220px. An item may have no note.

```sketch spectrum
title: The electromagnetic spectrum
ends: Long waves, low energy .. Short waves, high energy
Radio
Microwave: phones, ovens
Infrared: felt as heat
*Visible*: 380–750 nm
Ultraviolet: sunburn
X-ray: passes soft tissue
Gamma: nuclear decay
```

What a screen reader hears

As in skt.1, the figure is named “Sketch: title”, or “Sketch” with no title. Each kind adds its frame of reference after the title: the two names, the two sets, “shares of a whole”, the range, the two axes or the two ends. The items follow as a list in reading order, built from the items the sketch module returns.
