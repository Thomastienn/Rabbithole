A search window over every conversation

Rabbithole-nav.2. Search used to find learning tree titles only. Ctrl+K or the sidebar's Search button now opens a floating window that searches every conversation's title, passage, question and replies as the learner types. Results are grouped by learning tree. Spec: docs/search-window-version-and-map-cleanup-spec.md, "Search window". Where this mockup and the spec differ, follow the spec.

  • The window is centred, 760px wide, and at most 80% of the viewport tall. It shares the overlay frame and sits on a flat scrim. Below 48rem it spans the width less 16px on each side.
  • The field has no frame. Its underline turns porcelain while it holds focus. Focus stays in the field while Up and Down move the highlighted result.
  • The current learning tree's group comes first, marked "This tree". Then the other trees follow, newest first.
  • Each result shows its address chip in its line colour, its conversation title, and where the words matched. A snippet of about 160 characters follows, with the matched words marked in the selection colour.
  • A title match has no snippet, because the title already shows the words.
  • Typing waits 120 ms, then replaces the results in place. The old results stay until the new ones arrive, so the list never flashes empty.
  • The count line is a polite live region, for example "9 results in 3 learning trees". The selected result is announced as the active option.

1.2What are hyphae?

Tutor · Claude · Opus 4

A hypha is a single thread of a fungus, a tube one cell wide. Thousands of them together make the mycelium, the web you see as white fuzz under a log.

A hypha lengthens only at its tip, the last few micrometres, where new wall is laid down. Everything behind the tip stays put.

Because a thread is so thin, it reaches pores no root can enter, and that is where much of the phosphorus sits.

Opening a result

Enter opens the conversation in the focused chat, scrolled to the matched message. The matched words keep the selection colour for about two seconds, then fade. A title or passage match opens at the top. Escape instead closes the window and returns focus to where it was.
