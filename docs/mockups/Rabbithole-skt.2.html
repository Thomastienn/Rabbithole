Sketches: structure kinds

Rabbithole-skt.2 adds cycle, stack, tree, timeline and parts to the sketch module. They draw in replies exactly as flow and bars do in Rabbithole-skt.1: same place, title, focus, controls, placeholder and hiding. Spec: docs/sketches-spec.md. Decision: ADR 0027. The spec wins where they differ.

  • Everything in the skt.1 rules holds: no frame or fill, at most 640px wide, 8px below the paragraph before and 20px above the next, ink, muted ink, the strong seam and porcelain only. Lines are 3px strong seam with round caps and turn only at 45 or 90 degrees. Stations are 12px muted rings (radius 6, 2.5px stroke). Chevrons are 2px muted, 10px tall. Labels are 14px, notes 13px muted, the title 15px/600, all in the reading face.
  • The focus marks an item. Its ring fills porcelain at 14px (radius 7), or its shelf turns porcelain. Only the starred words turn porcelain at weight 600; the rest of a label keeps its colour. A label that is wholly starred is wholly porcelain.
  • Cycle is a loop line with two rows 84px apart, like a flow fork. The first half of the stages runs left to right along the top (the odd one goes on top), and the rest runs right to left along the bottom. Each row splits 640px into equal columns, with a ring centred in each. The loop runs 16px in from each edge, with 16px corners at 45 degrees. Each step has one muted chevron at its middle. The step from the last stage back to the first is the up chevron on the left side. Labels sit 26px below their ring and wrap at the column width minus 48px. Top labels stay inside the loop; up to three lines fit, and each further line pushes the bottom row 18px down.
  • Stack has one row per layer, with the first line on top. The name is 14px ink, up to 288px (45%) wide, and the note is 13px muted, 16px after the widest name, wrapping. Each layer sits on its own 3px round-capped strong-seam shelf, 6px below its text. Every shelf is as wide as the widest layer. Rows are 34px apart. A focus layer has a porcelain shelf.
  • Tree is an indented outline, one row per line, rows 28px apart; a wrapped label adds 18px. Each level, two spaces in the source, indents 24px. A parent's line drops straight down from its ring. Each child leaves that line with a 45 degree branch that starts 24px above the child's row and ends on its ring. Labels start 16px right of the ring. Lines at the top level stand alone, and no trunk is added to join them.
  • Timeline is one straight vertical line with one ring per line, rows 28px apart, in the order written. The app does not sort and does not space by date. The "when" column is right-aligned, as wide as its widest entry (at most 192px, 30%), and ends 18px left of the line. Events start 18px right of it and wrap at the column edge. There are no chevrons: time runs down.
  • Parts puts the whole on the left. Its label is right-aligned, at most 192px wide, and ends 16px before its ring. The ring sits level with the middle of the parts. Its line runs 24px to a vertical bus from the first part to the last, and each part leaves the bus at 90 degrees, 24px to its ring. Parts are rows 28px apart. Each part name is 14px ink, 16px after its ring. What it does is 13px muted, in a column 16px after the widest name.

In a reply: stack and timeline

Each sketch sits right after the paragraph it shows. At rest, as here, there are no controls until the sketch is pointed at.

Three languages, one kingdom

After 1066 England ran in three languages at once. Which one you heard depended on where you stood: in church, at court, or in the fields.

French sat in the middle, so its words leaked down into everyday English: beef, pork and mutton on the lord's table, cow, pig and sheep in the farmer's field.

How English won back the top

The French layer thinned once England lost Normandy, but the borrowing went on for two more centuries.

By 1400 English was back at the top, but it was a different English, carrying about ten thousand French words.

```sketch stack
title: Languages in Norman England
Latin: Church, scholarship, records
*French*: royal court and nobility
English: most of the population
```

```sketch timeline
title: French influence on English
1066: *Norman Conquest*
1200s: Borrowing from French surges
1362: English heard in law courts by statute
Late 1300s: Chaucer writes in English
1400s: English returns in government writing
```

In a reply: cycle and tree

The cycle's top labels sit inside the loop, and the bottom labels sit below it. The tree nests the way the outline does.

The loop that never stops

The sun lifts water off the oceans as vapour. High up it cools into cloud, falls as rain or snow, and runs back downhill to the sea to start again.

Evaporation is the engine. It is the one step that needs energy, and the sun supplies all of it.

Where rain goes after it lands

Rain on land does not all reach a river. Some sinks in, some is held as ice, and some rises straight back into the air.

Groundwater is the slow lane. A drop can stay underground for centuries before a spring brings it back.

```sketch cycle
title: The basic loop
*Evaporation*
Condensation
Precipitation
Collection and flow
```

```sketch tree
title: Where water can go on land
Precipitation
  Runs off into streams and rivers
  Soaks into the ground
    Taken up by plants
    Becomes *groundwater*
  Stored as snow or ice
  Evaporates again
```

In a reply: parts

The whole and its parts, with the focus words inside a part's note.

The second line of defence

If germs get past the skin and the first, general responders, a slower and more precise team takes over. It learns the exact shape of the invader, and it remembers it.

Antibodies are why a vaccine works: the team meets a harmless copy first, so it is ready the day the real germ arrives.

```sketch parts
title: The targeted team
whole: Adaptive immunity
Helper T cells: coordinate the others
B cells: lead to cells making *antibodies*
Killer T cells: destroy infected cells
```

Stack: seven layers, two in focus

Each line's name is kept as written, number included. Two starred layers both get a porcelain shelf. Only the starred words turn porcelain.

```sketch stack

6 Presentation: formats and encoding
5 Session: manages conversations

```

Tree: six levels deep

Tree: several top-level lines, no title
