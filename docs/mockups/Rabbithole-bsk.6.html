Board sketches: compare, sequence and equation build piece by piece

Rabbithole-bsk.6. The last three kinds of the chat's sketches come to the board, on the pattern of Rabbithole-bsk.4: a comparison, a message sequence and a labelled equation. The tutor gives each one a box; each child op adds one piece, one sentence at a time, and the sketch reflows the earlier pieces inside the box. The arrangement follows the chat sketch of the same name; every piece is drawn in the board's strokes, fonts and theme colours. Spec: docs/board-sketches-spec.md (Sketch kinds as board components). The spec wins where they differ.

  • Sizes are board units on the fixed 1600×900 board. Stations, lines, labels, notes, looks and reflow timing are Rabbithole-bsk.4's: 14-radius stations with a 5-unit ink edge, 4-unit muted lines, 32-unit ink labels, 28-unit muted notes.
  • Compare: the first column is as wide as the widest aspect name plus 48, at least 208; the two value columns share the rest equally. Side names are 32-unit semibold ink, 44 below the box's top, over 3-unit border-emphasized rules 70 below it, the left rule stopping 40 short of the right column. Rows start 80 below the top, at most 96 apart, with a 2-unit border rule between rows. Aspect names are 28-unit muted notes; values are 32-unit labels at each column's left.
  • Sequence: actors sit in equal columns, names 32-unit semibold ink 32 below the box's top. Lifelines are 3-unit border-emphasized rules from 60 below the top to 10 past the last message, at least 140 below the top. Message rows start 90 below the top, at most 100 apart. An arrow is a 4-unit muted line at 68% down its row with a 14-unit chevron, its 30-unit text centred 20 above it; a lifeline it crosses breaks 14 above and 10 below it in the board's ground. A note is a 12-radius station on its actor's lifeline with its text 30 to the right, or to the left for the last actor.
  • Equation: the formula sits 110 below the box's top, centred, 28 between tokens: 72-unit math italic, operators upright, superscripts 44 units raised 30. A meaning is a 3-unit muted leader from 24 below the formula down to its row, then 40 right, with 30-unit ink text 56 right of the symbol's centre. Meaning rows start 80 below the formula, 72 apart; the rightmost annotated symbol takes the top row.
  • Past eight children, text shrinks by 26/32 (32 to 26 units; 30 to about 24 for messages), stations to an 11 radius and note stations to about 10, and rows tighten; the box stays the same size.
  • A child the board cannot draw (no such sketch, another kind, another page, a missing argument, an actor not in the sequence, a symbol not in the equation, a compare row without one value per side) is left out without any notice on the board.
  • A sentence whose ops draw an equation or its symbols is spoken at the math rate, about -10%; a key sentence takes the slower key rate instead, never the sum. The board looks the same either way.
  • Colours are the theme's: ink is text-primary, muted text-secondary, rules border and border-emphasized, the ground the board surface, porcelain rabbithole-paper. A theme or appearance switch redraws every sketch in the new colours.
  • Captions show the sentence being spoken. Frames read left to right, top to bottom. Dashed boxes on a board are mockup markers only.

What happens when I open a page?

Let's follow what happens when you open a page. First the browser asks DNS where example.com is. DNS answers with an address. Then the browser asks that server for the page. The server sends it back. And the browser draws it.

Board pages list, as the tutor reads it (not drawn):
- Opening a page (open): q1 sequence (Browser, DNS, Server): Where is example.com?, 93.184.215.14, GET /, The page, Draws it

2. Sequence: one message at a time

The sequence op writes the actors' names across the top and starts their lifelines. Each message is one sentence: its text writes in, then its arrow draws from the sender to the receiver, and every lifeline lengthens to 10 past it. Rows stay 100 apart; they only tighten when the box is full. An arrow that crosses another actor's lifeline breaks that lifeline around itself.

> Let's follow what happens when you open a page.
q1 sequence actors="Browser|DNS|Server" at 800,450 w=1200 h=700
> First the browser asks DNS where example.com is.
m1 send "Where is example.com?" by=Browser to=DNS in q1
> DNS answers with an address.
m2 send "93.184.215.14" by=DNS to=Browser in q1
…
> And the browser draws it.
m5 send "Draws it" by=Browser in q1

Op names and arguments are the spec's; the op parser owns their exact syntax and a later benchmark may tune them. A send whose by= or to= names an actor not in the sequence is skipped and logged, and draws nothing.

3. Compare: one aspect at a time

The compare op writes the two sides' names over their columns, each over its own rule. Each aspect is one sentence: a row with its muted name on the left and one value under each side. Rows stack down from the header at most 96 apart, so earlier rows stay where they are until the box is full.

> Bacteria and viruses are both germs, but very different.
c1 compare vs="Bacteria|Virus" at 800,450 w=1100 h=500
> A bacterium is about a micrometre across, a virus a tenth of that.
a1 aspect "Size" values="about 1 µm|about 0.1 µm" in c1
…
> So antibiotics work on bacteria, but not on viruses.
a4 aspect "Antibiotics work" values="yes|no" in c1

A row without exactly one value per side is skipped and logged, and draws nothing.

4. Equation: one meaning at a time

The equation op writes the formula centred near the top of the box, symbols in italic and operators upright. Each symbol child is one sentence: a leader drops from under its symbol and turns right into its meaning. The rightmost annotated symbol takes the top row, so when a symbol further right arrives the earlier meanings step down a row and no leader ever crosses a meaning. A sentence that draws the equation or any of its symbols is spoken at the slower math rate, about -10% (Rabbithole-bsk.2); nothing on the board shows it.

> Here is the most famous equation there is.
x1 equation "E = m c^2" at 800,450 w=1200 h=500
> E is energy.
y1 symbol "E" means="energy" in x1
> m is mass.
y2 symbol "m" means="mass" in x1
> And c is the speed of light.
y3 symbol "c" means="the speed of light" in x1

A symbol that is not one of the equation's symbols is skipped and logged, and draws nothing.

5. More than eight pieces still fit the box

Past eight children the message text drops from 30 to about 24 units, note stations from 12 to about 10 radius, and the rows tighten to share the same box. The box never grows and no piece leaves it.

6. Reaching one piece

Every aspect, message and meaning is a board item with its own id, so the tutor highlights, points at and erases one piece, and the learner circles one with the pen.
