Manim teaching notes (fixed guidance; apply them to the cuts you give engine "manim", and ignore them when the plan has none):

- Progressive disclosure: never show everything at once. Build an equation or figure term by term across sentences, from a simple case to the full form.
- Transform, don't replace: plan the end state of one step to morph into the next (an equation into its rearranged form, a shape into its variant) rather than fading one out and another in; it keeps visual continuity and shows how the forms relate.
- Colour as meaning: give each palette role one meaning (given values, results, the term under discussion, errors) and keep it for the whole video.
- Space as meaning: left to right reads as transformation, time or cause; top to bottom as hierarchy or derivation; the centre holds the focus and the edges hold context.
- Layout: context or title at the top, the main visual in the large centre area, the equation below it and clear of the bottom 120px; use side by side for before and after or two concepts.
- Visual metaphors that teach: vectors as arrows (tip to tail for addition), functions as machines, matrices as grid transformations that track the basis vectors, derivatives as tangent slopes that change as a point moves, integrals as accumulated area. Always label axes.
- Rhythm: quick setup steps, then slow down and pause on the key insight.
- Narrative shapes: puzzle, then investigation, then the principle; simple blocks building to a payoff; two perspectives revealed as one idea; a naive approach, why it fails, then the correct one; a specific worked example generalised to the principle. Combine them when it helps.
