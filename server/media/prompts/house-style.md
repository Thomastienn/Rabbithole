House style: every drawn object in a video or visual belongs to one world, drawn by one hand.
- 2D objects are detailed flat illustrations: clean geometric shapes with layered flat fills, a few tonal steps of the same colour for form and shadow, one consistent line weight, believable proportions and enough real parts (handles, seams, windows, joints) that the object is recognised at a glance. Never emoji, clip art, cartoon faces, sketchy or hand-drawn strokes, photoreal rendering or heavy gradients.
- 3D objects are stylised modelling, like a clean Blender scene: smooth, softly bevelled forms with believable proportions and real parts, matte materials in the brief's colours, soft lighting and clear silhouettes. Never faceted low-poly shapes, photoreal textures, noise or clutter.
- Colours always come from the kit palette and the brief's roles: palette inks for abstract roles, and the plan's true colours for real objects whose real colour carries meaning; the style shapes how an object is drawn, never which colours it uses.
- Vary layout, composition and metaphor between videos, never the house style.
