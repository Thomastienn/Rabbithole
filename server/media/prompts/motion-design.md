Motion design (loaded skill for any animation: d3 transitions, CSS transitions or keyframes):

- Checklist: name what the motion should make the learner notice before choosing properties; animate transform and opacity, never layout properties; do not change an important state by opacity alone; keep movement on natural arcs and no single unbroken move longer than a third of the container; keep at most a third of the elements moving at once and fewer than 20 animated elements; give primary, secondary and ambient layers; offset child elements 50–150 ms behind their parent; the same interaction always gets the same motion; it must still feel right on the hundredth viewing.
- Durations by element: micro-feedback 80–120 ms, button or toggle 120–180 ms, icon change 150–250 ms, card enter or exit 200–350 ms, dialog 300–400 ms, whole-view change 400–600 ms, dramatic reveal 600–1200 ms, ambient 2–20 s. Scale with distance (50 px ×0.8, 100 px ×1, 200 px ×1.3, 300 px ×1.5, full width ×1.8–2). An exit takes 65–75% of its entrance.
- Feedback latency: hover under 100 ms, press under 150 ms, drag start under 50 ms, release or settle 200–300 ms.
- Easing by direction: entrance ease-out, exit ease-in, on-screen move ease-in-out, looping sine, rotation and progress linear; never linear for other spatial movement. Standard curves: default on-screen cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1), entrance (0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1), exit (0.3, 0, 1, 1), gentle ambient (0.4, 0, 0.2, 1), playful settle (0.175, 0.885, 0.32, 1.275). In d3 use the matching d3.easeCubicOut, d3.easeCubicIn, d3.easeCubicInOut or d3.easeSinInOut.
- Stagger: 20–40 ms for a micro cascade, 50–100 ms standard; the whole stagger stays under 500 ms. Overshoot: none for errors, 2–5% for feedback, 5–10% for success.
- Accessibility: provide the prefers-reduced-motion alternative, never carry critical information by motion alone, and make anything longer than 5 s pausable.
